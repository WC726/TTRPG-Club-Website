Character Creation 101

Step 1: Ability Scores

The first step to creating a character is to choose your ability scores. This is done in three different ways. One way is when you roll a set of 4 six sided dice, and add the three highest results. The second way is to allocate 27 points to increase each stat from a starting number of 8. The final way is to choose which numbers from a predetermined set to use for each stat. These numbers are 15, 14, 13, 12, 10 and 8.

Side Note: If you use choose to use the 5.5e standard array, don't.


Step 2: Choosing a Race

The next step to creating a character is to choose what race they will be. This will determine a few things, including how they fit into the world they will be playing in, various size and speed scores, and what stats they can increase based on which race they choose. It will also give them a few racial abilities that you should write in the Features & Traits box on your character sheet.

Take note that in the newer edition of 5.5, racial increases to ability scores are edited, so that ones background determines the increases to their stats, not their race.


Step 3: Choosing a Background

In the 5th edition of DND, backgrounds determine two skill proficiencies, two tool and/or language proficiencies, and a small set of equipment. Use them as guiding prompts when creating your character's backstory.

In 5.5e, backgrounds will increase your ability scores, an origin feat, two skill proficiencies, two tool and/or language proficiences, and some equipment. The ability score increases work as follows: The background will give you an option of three abilities. You may choose to increase the score of one ability by 1 and another one by 1, or you may choose to increase all three of the ability scores by 1. Write the name of your origin feat in the Feats box.


Step 4: Choosing a Class

Choosing a class will determine what archetype your character will fit into within their party.

Tank specialized classes include the Barbarian and the Paladin. The Barbarian is a berserker, usually stylized with nordic cultural appropriations. The Paladin is a classic knight in shining armor, empowered by their holy oath to their god.

Classes specialized in dealing high melee damage include the Fighter, Rogue, Ranger and the Monk.
The Fighter is a soldier, with room to specialize in a range of different niches.
The Rogue is the stealthiest and the most fragile melee class. Their combat abilities are designed around striking foes from the shadows and escaping quickly. They have several abilities to reduce damage, but cannot function as tanks.
The Ranger functions similarly to the Marvel hero Hawkeye, relying on ranged combat with a bow and arrow. Out of combat, they are stylized as a frontiersman/woodland protector, utilizing stealth and scant magical ability to protect the woods they call home.
The Monk is your culturally appropriated martial arts master, punching things a lot and being really hard to land a blow on. In a pinch, they can act as a Tank, but it is not recommended because if you hit them, they aren't likely to survive.

The Spellcasting classes specialized to do damage consist of the Warlock, the Wizard and the Sorcerer. The Warlock is built around their deal with a higher power, gaining a range of different abilities determined by their patron.
The Sorcerer gains their powers from a magical ancestor, ranging from fairies, dragons, angels and more.
Wizards learn their magic from tireless work and diligence, specializing in certain disciplines of magic.

Bards are spellcasters who primarily specialize in support, buffing their allies and hindering enemies. However, they can also take on the roles of healer, damage caster, and even melee fighter depending on their specialization, although they cannot tank. Their flavor is the magical performer, who casts magic through musical performances.

Clerics are traditionally healers, and they are the best healing class by a decent margin. However, their diverse range of subclasses allow them to take on any role in a party. They are holy priests, devoted to a god of your choice. Depending on what domain their god controls, they get a variety of different abilities.

Druids are nature-themed spellcasters, and they have the ability to shapeshift into animals. Like Clerics, Druids can take on any role in a party, however, their specializations are much more flexible, allowing them to switch between roles with relative ease.
